# Teacher Prep Pack — Lesson 01 PowerPoint Project ("Slide Squad")

**IC3 Digital Literacy Level 1 · Lesson 01: Technology Basics · Medina County Career Center**

---

## What this project is

Students haven't touched PowerPoint yet, and Lesson 01 is the one topic the jigsaw project (topics 02–07) doesn't cover. This project does both jobs at once: six teams each take one sub-lesson of `dl1_01_Slides.md` (01a–01f), and rebuild it as a 6-slide PowerPoint deck — same IC3-tested facts, expanded with research, built to a specific PowerPoint skill spec. The six decks combine into one class study deck for the Level 1 exam.

The PowerPoint requirements are deliberately a beginner subset of **MO-310 (MOS PowerPoint Associate)** skills, so this is also day one of the MOS PowerPoint pathway without announcing it as such.

## Introducing PowerPoint: the four demos

Run these as live demos with students following along on their own machines, each one right before students need that skill. Each maps to your MOS unit if you want the click-by-click reference. No timing prescribed — insert them where they fit your calendar.

**Demo 1 — A deck exists (before teams start building).**
New presentation → apply a design theme (Design tab) → why layouts beat floating text boxes (New Slide dropdown: Title Slide vs. Title and Content) → save with a real filename. Have everyone produce their titled, themed, correctly-named 1-slide deck before leaving.
*Reference: `MOS_PowerPoint/ppt_01_managePresentations`, `ppt_02_slides` walkthroughs.*

**Demo 2 — Text that behaves (before content slides).**
Bulleted lists; Tab / Shift+Tab for indent levels; Insert > Table, adding rows, resizing; why 30 words on a slide is already too many.
*Reference: `ppt_03_textShapesImages` walkthrough (text portions).*

**Demo 3 — Pictures done right (before the image slides).**
Insert > Pictures; corner-handle resizing vs. edge-handle squishing (show the crime, then the fix); alt text (right-click > View Alt Text) — say why it exists, not just where it is; where the image source goes. Then: Insert > Header & Footer → slide number + footer + the "Don't show on title slide" checkbox — the exact kind of detail MO-310 tests.
*Reference: `ppt_03_textShapesImages` walkthrough (images).*

**Demo 4 — Motion with restraint (before polish/presenting).**
One transition applied to all slides (Transitions > Apply To All); entrance animation on the quiz answer (Animations tab, On Click); demonstrate an over-animated slide as the cautionary tale; F5 / Shift+F5, advancing, B for black screen.
*Reference: `ppt_05_transitionsAnimations` walkthrough (basics only — save the Animation Pane, triggers, and Morph for the MOS unit proper).*

## The fact-check gate

Before a team presents, check their must-include facts against their spec sheet (each sheet bolds every tested term). Errors go back for fixes — nothing wrong enters the class study deck. This is the highest-leverage grading moment: a wrong definition studied by 20 students is negative teaching.

## Assembling the class study deck (your MO-310 party trick)

After presentations, assemble all six decks into one — **live, on the projector**, using **Home > New Slide > Reuse Slides**, pulling each team's slides 2–5 into a master deck. Two payoffs: the class watches a real MOS exam skill (Reuse Slides + the "Keep source formatting" checkbox — a known MO-310 gotcha), and the formatting chaos when six themes collide is your opening line for why the Slide Master exists when you hit `ppt_01` for real. Post the combined deck as Lesson 01 study material.

## Rubric (50 points — mirrors the jigsaw rubric)

| Criterion | Points | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Must-include facts all present and exactly correct | 15 | Check against the team's spec sheet; IC3 wording preserved |
| Go-deeper research | 10 | Actually done, credible source on slide 6, explained in their own words |
| PowerPoint requirements (all 9 from the brief) | 15 | Theme, layouts, 2-level list, real table, image w/ alt text + source, slide numbers/footer (not on title), one consistent transition, animated reveal, correct filename |
| Presentation | 5 | Every member spoke; quiz moment ran ask → answers → reveal |
| Sources slide | 5 | Facts and images both covered |

**Quick PPT checklist per deck (score the 15):** ☐ theme ☐ correct layouts ☐ 2-level bullets ☐ Insert>Table table ☐ image not distorted ☐ alt text present ☐ image source on slide 6 ☐ slide numbers + footer, suppressed on title ☐ one transition on all slides ☐ entrance animation on reveal, fires on click ☐ filename `DL1_[sub]_[names].pptx`

## Go-deeper answer key (so you can sanity-check fast)

- **01a**: Web invented by Tim Berners-Lee, 1989–91 at CERN; first popular browser Mosaic (1993). First mass-market graphical desktop: Apple Macintosh, 1984 (Xerox Alto/Star earlier but not consumer).
- **01b**: Any verified real product facts are fine; watch for "improved" (broken) definitions.
- **01c**: "First personal computer" has defensible answers — Altair 8800 (1975, ~$439 kit), Apple II (1977, ~$1,298), IBM PC (1981, ~$1,565). Accept any with a source; the comparison sentence is the point.
- **01d**: USB = Universal Serial Bus, introduced 1996, replaced the serial/parallel/PS-2 port zoo. EU common-charger rule: USB-C required on phones sold in the EU from late 2024.
- **01e**: Pre-OS: toggle switches, punch cards, one program at a time. Pre-Windows OSes: CP/M, MS-DOS, Apple DOS, UNIX. "Daemon" comes from Maxwell's demon via MIT's Project MAC, not "demon."
- **01f**: ARPANET first connected UCLA and Stanford in 1969; opened commercially early 1990s. `8.8.8.8` is Google Public DNS — memorable, reliable, so it became the universal "is the Internet up" ping target.
