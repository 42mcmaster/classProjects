## Slide 3 — "A Browser Is Not the Internet"

**Must-include facts (exact):**
- A web browser **retrieves and presents information from the World Wide Web**.
- A browser is **not** the Internet, and a browser is **not** a search engine.
- Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox = **browsers**. Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo = **search engines** you open *inside* a browser.
- A **URL** is the address that identifies the location of information on the Web.

**Go deeper (research required, cite your source):**
- A short history of the Web: who invented the World Wide Web, and in what year? What was the first popular web browser called?

**PowerPoint requirement:** a **table** (Insert > Table) sorting at least 6 named programs/sites into Browser vs. Search Engine.

## Slide 4 — "One Login, Many Doors: SSO"

**Must-include facts (exact):**
- **Single Sign-On (SSO)**: log in once with one set of credentials, reach many connected services.
- All three tested benefits: remember only **one set of login details**; access email, storage, and apps after one sign-in; **stronger security** because a trusted authenticator manages your credentials.
- One thing SSO is **not** (pick from the guide: not government-owned; doesn't mean no password).

**Go deeper:**
- A real example from your own life: what does your school SSO unlock after one sign-in? List the actual services.

**PowerPoint requirement:** an inserted **picture** (e.g., a "Sign in with Google/Microsoft" button screenshot or a key/door image) — resized without distortion, **alt text** added, source cited on slide 6.
